# Ordinary armor prices and chainmail production allocation

**Status:** AUTHOR-ESTABLISHED MK163 working reference; qualifications below apply.

## Leather armor averages

| Non-enchanted configuration | Average retail eln | Artisan example, not a cap |
|---|---:|---|
| Light leather | 50 | Unassigned |
| Medium leather | 110 | Approximately 200–300 |
| Full leather | 180 | Approximately 300–400; around 400 at the upper example |
| Standard well-crafted chainmail shirt | 200 | Customized variants unassigned |

These are MK163 working equipment categories, not claims of standardized medieval classes. Fit, coverage, quality, materials, artistry and workmanship affect an actual commission; district identity does not fix price. Aesthetic craftsmanship does not by itself establish better protection. All benchmarks exclude Ain/enchanting, enhancement resources and special magical effects. Ordinary material and assembly are included.

The unchanged LEATHER_ARMOR_AVERAGES.svg depicts 50/110/180/200 eln, not protective performance or historical prices. A standard shirt can be worn under full leather: 200 + 180 = 380 eln average base purchase, before extra fitting, underlayers or enhancements.

## Standard chainmail shirt

The author explicitly selected 200 eln after discussion of fourteenth-century English mail and modeled wages. This is an MK163 decision, not a historical exchange-rate derivation. The former 150–300-eln speculation is not the benchmark. A standard shirt equals two months of Red gross reference income, one month Orange, or half a month Yellow; actual savings depend on expenses.

- Standard shirt: **23,000 prefabricated rings**.
- Finished retail benchmark: **200 eln**.
- Bulk ring batch: **80 eln**, 40% of retail.
- Remaining **120 eln**, 60%, covers assembly, fitting, finishing, incidental business costs and commercial margin. It is **not net profit**.
- Derived unit equivalent: 80/23000 ≈ 0.00347826 eln per ring; at 180 ahm/eln this is ≈ 0.626087 ahm. This is not a mandated individual-ring retail price.

Kaira buys finished rings from her Blacksmith Row supplier and manually assembles an ordinary, high-quality demonstration shirt. The supplier-pricing amendment supersedes the earlier wholly-open production-input statement. Actual net profit, assembly duration, incidental expenses, repair fees, negotiated commissions and enchanted/custom variants remain open. The figure does not price the future Chainmail Robe.
