## Manufacturing and identity rules
- **Mandatory mass reduction:** all marketed robe tiers include the 150-eln standard sigil service, irrespective of layer count. The completed garment is placed in the sigil anchor basin and enchanted. This is a standardized price/model, not proof of identical energy throughput or every engineering detail.
- One-layer garments are low-end imitations; they may need clothing underneath. More layers generally improve coverage/opacity and raise materials and labor. Kaira's five-layer original is intended to be opaque, close-fitting, lightweight, smooth, washable, rust-resistant and wearable without underclothes. Layer count alone does not guarantee these properties or reproduce her interlayer weave.
- Lower-priced robes permit Red/Orange and other residents to participate in the emerging Transductionist dress identity. Inspired imitations and fraudulent counterfeit claims are distinct; no new intellectual-property enforcement regime is established here.
- Ring count: **not yet canonically fixed** for the complete five-layer original. Equal-per-layer approximation N(L)=L/5 * N(5) is illustrative only; no absolute ring count or finished engineering recipe is invented.
- Margin is a price component, not assured net profit; overhead, fees, material sourcing, fit, weave complexity and market variation remain open.

# OR-02 — Staging economy: 1.3–1.7 locality premium, resource wealth, repair markets

**Orange arc of Book Red · 2026-11-08.** ## Relative economic levels

Let **C** be the price charged for a *comparable* item/service/housing class in the nearby large city. The **large city baseline = 1.0 × C**. In the Violet Dungeon staging settlement, **normal comparable pricing is 1.3–1.7 × C**, a **30–70% location premium**. The user expressly prefers that this is a **broad defining economic rule**, not an inn-only surcharge or casual suggestion: food, lodging, residences, ordinary consumer purchases, repair services, shop/workshop rent, other local professional services and their local operating expenses participate in the premium, with actual prices determined by property, quality, scarcity, competition and other trade circumstances. This is a comparative rule rather than a mandated exact price for every SKU: do not mechanically overwrite historic equipment benchmarks or create impossible identical prices for noncomparable goods.

Why: the staging settlement is immediately convenient to the dungeon. Travelers could stay in the main city at ordinary rates, but commuting must traverse the city—**10 miles × 10 miles total footprint**—and its gate before continuing the roughly **five miles** to the staging area. An illustrative expedition participant might travel ten miles in a day just to deliver damaged armor or buy supplies, depending on city position and streets. These are examples, not locked route distances. Persistent vaelum/research parties with frequent dungeon work pay a premium to save time, maintain immediate readiness and avoid unnecessary trips.

## Dungeon-generated purchasing power and consumption

The Violet Dungeon naturally replenishes valuable commodities through its Genesis processes. Expeditions bring out **Genesis Dust from defeated ELDRIS**, **Genesis crystals**, naturally occurring **vaen** with relevant identities, and other ordinary or Genesis-influenced flora and materials. Materials enter local markets, professional contracts, commissioned manufacturing, alchemy and wider trade. Some groups sell in the staging settlement and use their revenues immediately for inns, restaurants, tools, repairs, provisioning, supplies and entertainment before descending again. Others transport materials into the city and beyond. The settlement can host wealth and high turnover without implying every resident or aithren is affluent.

There are legitimate alternatives: rent lower-cost city housing or larger premium staging residences; commute or stay local; purchase same-day local repair vs send work to a cheaper city contractor. Frequent expedition travel explains why a premium is economically sustainable. It is **not** a rule that each local business earns guaranteed profit or that skilled workers receive matching 1.3–1.7 wage raises.
