# Dimensional Rings and Bracelets — mechanism, access and relative capacity

**Project:** MK163  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED / INHERITED WITH AUTHOR AMENDMENTS  
**Canonical path:** `WORLD_CLOCK/L2_WORLD_CIVILIZATION/TECHNOLOGY/DIMENSIONAL_STORAGE.md`

The Dimensional Ring master is preserved in full. Its core is recursive-topology conservation and inheritance reassignment, **not** a pocket dimension, inventory menu, database or literal interior warehouse volume. An object continues to exist with identity, structure, relationships and information conserved.

Storage: **Expand → Select → Collapse → Conserve.** Supply energy, expand accessible configuration space, select a reachable object/topology and resolve its inheritance from environment to ring. Retrieval: **Recognize → Select → Collapse → Retrieve.** Conscious recognition selects the object's topological identity and restores inheritance to the environment. User familiarity matters; the Jenkins principle distinguishes a disorganized user's wrong retrieval from device failure.

Projection/reach and storage capacity are separate. The source's illustrative quality-radius sequence is Red 3 ft, Orange 5 ft, Yellow 7 ft, Green 11 ft, Blue 17 ft, Violet 23 ft, White 31+ ft. The definitive source calls these adjustable examples, despite older compact MK147 wording labeling them locked. Do not silently turn reach into cubic-foot capacity.

The selected common wearable forms are rings and bracelets, with a bangle as one bracelet style. Both have generous capacity. A bracelet generally carries about two to three times a ring's capacity because of its structural form. This is a relative practical capacity statement, not a derived volume law or proof that every bracelet exceeds every exceptionally high-quality ring. No fixed cubic feet, mass ceiling or item count is specified.

They are established expensive technologies, not newly invented by Kaira. Substantial resources and specific Genesis crystal arrangements make manufacture cost-prohibitive for ordinary laborers and beginners. Nobles, rich merchants and affluent professionals may own them; a merchant carrying valuable stock may prefer a bracelet. The materials need not all be intrinsically rare for the complete assembly to be costly.

Kaira uses ordinary packs while adventuring throughout most of Book Red and learns to pack light. Only at the very end, after her team's fourth-place Green tournament result, does she receive an exceptionally good **ring**, not a bracelet. From Orange through White it holds travel supplies/personal possessions and frees mobility. It does not create supplies, replace preparation or automatically grant combat powers. Exact grade and full equipment rules remain unassigned.
