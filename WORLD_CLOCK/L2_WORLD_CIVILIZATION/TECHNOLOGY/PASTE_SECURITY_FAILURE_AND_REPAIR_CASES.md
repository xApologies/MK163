# Alchemical paste: authorized repair, accidental damage, criminal misuse, and civic consequences

## Why the material was introduced
The author confronted the practical question of what happens if someone takes a sledgehammer to a phase-grown stone wall or destroys a section of carved sigil geometry. Replacing an entire wall, building block or installed alchemical table because of one localized defect would be wasteful and implausible. Alchemical paste is the simple repair medium that restores damaged matter. It is a paste before activation, can be pressed or smeared into cracks and carved channels, and after appropriate Transduction it bonds to the existing substrate and assumes that substrate's matter identity. On stone it becomes compatible stone. On another material, the same principle applies, although exact substrate restrictions and preparation steps remain open.

The paste does not magically remember the original sigil pattern. If a rune groove was erased, the repaired surface becomes a blank workable area; a qualified runesmith or architect must reproduce the correct geometry and verify that the larger array is functioning. This preserves professional competence and avoids turning paste into a universal auto-healing spell.

## Why availability is controlled
The user was concerned that a child could acquire ordinary paste and seal every sigil on a city wall, rendering an entire civilization's utilities vulnerable to casual pranks. The selected solution combines production cost, professional Transduction requirements, restricted distribution and activation controls. The paste requires an alchemical table, valuable Genesis crystals, a specialist operator and a controlled production sequence. It is not an ordinary sealant sold beside cheap household paint. Exact chromatic recipe was deliberately left open: a mix involving Red, Orange and Green or all six colors was floated as examples. The final formula should not be invented here.

The city can require certified professional possession. Authorized architects, runesmiths, approved civic maintenance personnel and regulated industrial facilities may store and use paste. The White noble who administers the city may oversee its licensing, although the exact legal code and kingdom-wide jurisdiction remain open. Unauthorized possession can itself be punishable, with serious fines and potential jail. A one-month term was an illustration, not a fixed universal sentence. The analogy to regulated firearms or hazardous industrial chemicals is a social-control analogy, not a requirement to reproduce modern United States legislation.

## Theft and realistic civic incidents
Control is not perfect. A few adolescents could steal three buckets from a warehouse and use the paste to fill grooves on civic stonework. They could disrupt a fountain or other public installation. This is a plausible background event in a large living city, not an approved Arc Two plot point. Its value is that it introduces ordinary vandalism, guards, complaints, repairs and municipal budgets. It makes a clean city feel inhabited by imperfect people rather than immune to human behavior.

Some sigil arrays may tolerate local scratches or partial channel obstruction because the overall topology is redundant or resilient. The author stated that a detailed anti-vandalism architecture had been worked out in earlier material, but it was not recovered in this workshop. Therefore do not claim every scratched rune disables the entire network or that every array is invulnerable. The exact threshold of failure remains an AERA engineering question.

## Case: damaged alchemical table
An alchemist overdrives an anchor by setting the Red and Green energy controls too high or activating them in the wrong order. The Transduction becomes locally unstable and damages a rune channel or anchor seat. The table ceases to perform its intended mode reliably. The alchemist identifies the failed process. An authorized runesmith applies alchemical paste to restore the substrate, re-carves the damaged portion of the sigil, and tests the table. This is a repair cycle, not an automatic total table replacement. Proper operation need not cause chronic erosion; accidental overdrive explains repairs despite durable stone.

## Case: wall damage
A vandal smashes an exposed stone block carrying part of a sigil. A maintenance crew assesses whether the active array remains stable, isolates the damaged segment if needed, fills the missing stone with paste, restores the carved geometry, and validates the utility. The surrounding wall can remain in place. This explains how a city made of standardized phase-grown blocks remains maintainable over decades or centuries.

## Industrial consequences
A small number of authorized alchemical producers may manufacture paste in bulk. They may be specialized alchemical houses or broader Transduction-equipment manufacturers; this is not yet decided. Cost, storage and professional access generate a market for licensing, supply contracts, repairs, auditing and insurance-like risk arrangements if later developed. No new company name, patent law, price list, or mandatory separate 'paste guild' should be invented. Civic maintenance is one of the three approved new city-map functions and should be linked to White civic authority and distributed service crews.
