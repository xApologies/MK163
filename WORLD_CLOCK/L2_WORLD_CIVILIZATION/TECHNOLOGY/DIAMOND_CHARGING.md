# Diamond charging rates, concurrency, labor and infrastructure

**Project:** MK163  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED / INHERITED WITH AUTHOR AMENDMENTS  
**Canonical path:** `WORLD_CLOCK/L2_WORLD_CIVILIZATION/TECHNOLOGY/DIAMOND_CHARGING.md`

Reference times are for one empty 1/4-inch chip charged to Red using **one basin alone**: Red 8 active hours, Orange 7, Yellow 5, Green 3, Blue 2, Violet 1. They do not specify every practitioner's exact endurance or all configurations.

Concurrent access to several basins can increase energy delivery and charge faster. Red+Orange+Green and all-six charging are explicitly allowed examples for capable practitioners. Do not add those times or automatically sum reciprocals without an agreed throughput/acceptance/fatigue model. The diamond must organize incoming energy; it cannot be forced instantly from empty to Violet by merely naming a high basin.

Personal charging can be supplemental labor: one hour nightly using Red accumulates eight active hours across eight days. A person may charge while walking or performing a job that does not otherwise use their Genesis access, such as a barista. A sufficiently capable person can charge professionally. Fatigue, available time, basin control and channeling limits prevent unlimited output. Charging duration, work schedule and endurance are separate.

Infrastructure uses sigil arrays to collect Genesis energy into batteries continuously, analogous to solar collection/storage but not requiring sunlight. Real installations charge one-foot-cube or larger blocks for large-scale Transduction, not ordinarily individual chips. When a charge cycle deactivates, an operator can replace the diamond and reactivate the apparatus.

The **five-day** chip-equivalent benchmark shows infrastructure's slow accumulation rate. The author's five-hours/Yellow-speed passage is corrected later in the same utterance back to five days and agrees with the Diamond Master. Do not extrapolate five days directly into a full one-foot-block charging time. Nor convert five local days into 120 local hours: under the inherited 31-hour day that would be 155 local hours if the benchmark is interpreted on the local clock; the original “day” unit context remains in its source. The earlier assistant's 120-hour conversion was Earth-day arithmetic, not adopted canon.

The important economic contrast is continuous slow infrastructure versus faster active personal work with limited available hours and fatigue. Some earnings can be exchanged at a bank/diamond exchange for eln, Genesis Dust or other commodities, or saved as energy reserves. Detailed rates are separate from these charging benchmarks.
