# Operational model of sigil-guided alchemy — detailed author workshop record

## Core distinction: ingredients, apparatus, topology, conductance
An alchemist does not simply throw colored crystals into a beaker and invoke a magic word. The professional is a material scientist who arranges apparatus and resources at specific points on a rune-covered working table. The table is an installed geometric instrument: a large stone working surface covered by an integrated sigil, which may contain distinct anchor locations, channels and interaction points. The container holding the product, whether a beaker, flask, crucible or other apparatus, has a defined target anchor. Chromatic crystals occupy their own anchor points and supply available energy for the preparation. Additional vaen or other materials occupy separate positions as specified by the formulation. The sigil establishes the topology under which their energy can be guided into the transformation. The process may involve stages, and the operator actively controls the transition rather than treating the table as an unattended automatic machine.

The user gave a concrete example: a Red crystal at one anchor, a Green crystal at another, and a potion vessel at its own anchor. A projected tactile surface could be slid like a recording-studio fader. Sliding the Red control down from full to half requests reduced energy-transfer throughput; sliding Green toward two-thirds requests a different rate. The crystals already define the total energy budget the recipe will consume by completion. The control is about the temporal profile of that consumption, including which basin contributes when, whether energy enters quickly or slowly, and how the field remains stabilized as it receives the energy. The operator might need to guide one stream first, stabilize, then open another stream. One incorrect transfer schedule may produce an unusable preparation or physically damage channels.

The conventional physics vocabulary is energy transfer rate or power. In ordinary mathematical notation P_i(t)=dE_i/dt, and total transferred E_i=integral(P_i(t)dt) over the operating interval. This is a useful analogy for tracking time and throughput, not a derivation of AERA Genesis mathematics or an assertion of linear slider calibration. 'Halfway' on a tactile slider can represent an operator request; it is not proof the fictional conduit actually transmits exactly half the energy without control feedback. A wrong configuration might put Red at full when intended at half and Green at full when intended at roughly two-thirds. The resulting mismatch can destabilize a local energy corridor or the rune geometry it occupies.

## Universal seven-position design
The author proposed a standard general-purpose table with seven main crystal anchor positions: Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Violet and White. White is the superposed six-basin structure, not an independent seventh chromatic energy basin. A preparation may leave some crystal positions unused. The standard geometry provides a versatile substrate for many different alchemical processes. Different products are possible by selecting modes or active anchor relationships, not by physically replacing the entire table for each recipe. A small table can have smaller anchor basins/seats and a handful of extra vaen anchors. A high-end laboratory table may have large crystal seats and many extra vaen anchors, enabling greater available energy budgets and more complex material organizations. The exact anchor dimensions and capacity rules are not yet set.

## ER foam human–machine interface
ER foam is a Genesis-field projection from sigils, stabilized and phase-locked into a tactile topology. The projected surface is slightly elastic and feels approximately like firm or medium-hard rubber. It is not a light-only hologram. In households it can make a roughly two-inch square switch to turn sigil lights on and off, or a tactile faucet control for hot/cold water. The same mechanism can project tactile topographic maps and specialized controls. A table mode changes which projected controls are presented: activation switch, slider, mode selector or other needed physical input. The permanent carved sigil remains unchanged; the selected operating configuration and projected ER foam interface change. This makes the instrument sophisticated without adding electronics, touchscreens or a modern computer. The high-fantasy appearance is still a stone rune-carved workbench with projected tactile fields.

## Standard versus proprietary modes
A general commercial table might offer a range of ordinary potion operations, restoration procedures, biological media processing and metal infusion. These are categories, not a locked recipe catalogue. A specialty house can design custom table geometries and modes that support its protected process. Even knowing the ingredients may be insufficient without the exact active topology, staged activation sequence, throughput profile, vaen placement and operator competency. This provides a meaningful reason for trade secrecy, in-house architects, and occasional industrial espionage. It does not establish a secret universal conspiracy.

## Failure, repair and industrial lifecycle
Stone sigils can be long-lived. Normal energy passage is not assumed to erode every rune. Incorrect operation can create localized instability along a corridor, channel or crystal anchor. The result may be cracked or degraded inscriptions and a temporarily unusable table, not necessarily catastrophic destruction. The user preferred this failure model over arbitrary age-related wear. A century was floated as possible table lifetime, but not canonized as an automatic expiry. The alchemist can identify which process failed; the architect/runesmith repairs the carved geometry, possibly with alchemical paste to restore substrate and then reinscribes the rune. Equipment supply chains include quarry/stone fabrication, professional sigil engineering, assembly, sales and repairs. Two or three kingdom-level manufacturers were suggested, names and exact market share open. There is no need for a crystal-specialist profession: miners collect crystals and alchemists process them as needed.

## Alchemical houses
An independent shop can produce routine formulas, while a large research house might employ ten to one hundred alchemists and its own architectural/sigil staff. The house guards recipes, sigil equations, table construction, activation sequences, yields and efficiencies. Like a proprietary industrial process, its protected knowledge can be embodied in documents and equipment. Organizations may compete to discover one another's formulations; the analogy is corporate/industrial espionage. Not every alchemist is hostile, but research practitioners can be private, absorbed in laboratory work, and protective of methods. Their professional identity as material scientists is distinct from the general population's tendency to think only of potions.

## The exceptional Awakening preparation
The Stone Hollow alchemist has ordinary low-grade healing potions, some medium-grade and perhaps one or two high-grade ones. Kaira's severe wounds are first treated with a healing potion that accelerates wound closure and leaves scar tissue. Then the alchemist must deal with extraordinary quantities of White crystal evidence. In the author's working formulation, the alchemist processes all recovered White crystals to fine dust in a fluid vessel; uses a Red-through-Violet collection as energy inputs and three rare black-mushroom vaen to help structure the unusual White-heavy transformation. The sigil is activated in stages, the crystals are consumed, their organized energy enters the fluid, and White superposition stabilizes the preparation. This is a working story-specific alchemical implementation; exact ratios, whether mushroom chemistry is required for all Awakening Potions, and the full sigil mathematics remain open. Do not universalize Kaira's exceptional potion as the standard commercial recipe.
