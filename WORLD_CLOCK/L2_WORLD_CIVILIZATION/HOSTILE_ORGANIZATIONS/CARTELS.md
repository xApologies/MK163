# Cartels — regional black markets and leverage

**Project:** MK163  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED / INHERITED WITH AUTHOR AMENDMENTS  
**Canonical path:** `WORLD_CLOCK/L2_WORLD_CIVILIZATION/HOSTILE_ORGANIZATIONS/CARTELS.md`

Cartels are regional criminal organizations focused on money, power, control and black-market access. They are not the same organizational type as bandit camps, a conquering army or a replacement sovereign. They seek durable income, resource access/control, debt leverage and monopolies rather than openly sitting on the throne.

The inherited source supports commercial dependencies, illicit markets, intermediaries, blackmail, kidnapping and deniable political pressure. Its distinction is indirect leverage against local power rather than open military confrontation with the entire Crown. Source references to hidden dependencies and institutional fragility establish narrative antagonism, not a requirement that every noble is corrupt.

Cartels connect regional buyers with illicit maritime suppliers. Pirates move restricted goods and trafficked people across kingdoms/continents; cartels arrange black-market transactions, distribution and contracts. The author's restricted blackwood example shows how each intermediary takes a share and prices rise. It is an example, not a newly named tree species, established export treaty or real-world smuggling instruction.

Cartel operations are socially visible through consequences before Kaira necessarily understands the full network. Orange and Yellow are the main early narrative engagement window; later presence can continue. Drug dependency, slavery and trafficking are not confined to one villain's on-page introduction.

The source's roughly eleven cartels across six kingdoms and exact neighbor-war numbers are old starting-region choices, not MK163 demographics. Specific names, leaders, symbols, territories and Crown intervention policies remain to be chosen. The complete original document is preserved with its original Sanctuary terminology; current active text does not install that older geography.
