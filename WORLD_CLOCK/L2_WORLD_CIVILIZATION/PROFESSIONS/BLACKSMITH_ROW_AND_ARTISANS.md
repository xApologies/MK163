# OR-05 — Smithy architecture: standard-depth modular bays, two tiers, horizontal growth

**Orange arc of Book Red · 2026-11-08.** ## Where and what the businesses are

The Violet Dungeon staging settlement's **southwest area** has craftsmen's workshops, smithies and markets. This is **not** the city's Yellow District Blacksmith Row: the large city has three elite Rows located in its Yellow District, while the staging settlement has distinct local working shops. The two places share stone/sigil architectural technology and possibly similar two-tier public plaza design, but their economic roles and professional prestige are not interchangeable. The settlement's smithies charge locality-premium prices, broadly 1.3–1.7 × comparable city costs. Established local customers bring used/partly damaged dungeon equipment. Not all smithies repair chainmail; families specialize in mailwrighting, plate/armor, swords/polearms, architectural metal, household goods, decorative one-offs, and so forth.

## Horizontal bays, open to the street

Smithies have an **approximately fixed depth of 30–40 feet**. A small smithy occupies **one open-fronted bay**, a medium shop **two adjoining bays**, a large shop **three adjoining bays**. These are working design classifications, not a required count or exact width in feet. A multi-bay smithy is one business occupying a larger combined width, **not** three identical individual smiths. Businesses can expand by acquiring frontage rather than growing endlessly into deep halls. Individual bay frontage width, structural partition locations, ceiling clearance and specific store geometry remain OPEN. The public-facing bays can be closed with a projected **invisible security barrier** activated by a sigil at night. Houses may have doors; commercial space can be open-faced while operating.

The author gave an illustrative three-bay weaponsmith arrangement: **Bay 1—processing, raw/finished stock, storage; Bay 2—sigil-based forge and fabrication; Bay 3—customer-facing presentation, finished weapons, inspection and commission negotiation**. This is an example of **operational zones**, not a mandatory floor plan and not permission to create detached factories. A customer can see actual artisans at work without a false show-only demonstration.

## Two-story terrace relationship

An open public plaza separates **two facing rows** of smithies (one on each side). Each row can rise to **two stories**, with a recessed upper tier:

- **Ground-floor full depth: ~40 feet** (working selection).
- **Second-floor enclosed shop depth: ~20 feet**, toward the rear.
- **Elevated promenade: ~20 feet deep**, occupying the frontal half of the ground-floor roof, so the upper shop wall is set back by twenty feet.
- **Total base footprint remains ~40 feet** deep. The promenade is recessed atop the lower structural volume; **NO OVERHANG** or cantilevered front gallery extending above the plaza.
- Ground-level bays open directly to plaza. An elevated promenade allows customers to browse the upper-level shopfronts as a second commercial tier. Upper tenants need not have the same owner/bay boundaries as ground level.
- Plaza central width, railing specifications, access stairs/elevator placements, open bay width, shop height and tenancy count remain OPEN.

Shared architecture uses precisely formed phase-grown stone, spatial locking, subdued trim/inscriptions, maege lighting and maintained floors. Different shops develop distinctive interiors, signs, material racks, sample work, layout and craftsmanship, while their structural shells share a visual language. The user's image experiments that showed **four shallow identical bays** or a long alley with inward-looking perspective were rejected as not differentiated/too small. The more successful later three-bay front-on renderings are **concepts only**, not measurements or final names.

## Correct functional aesthetic: smiths as conductors of matter

A working smithy is clean, well organized, oriented to actual material Transduction and hand finishing, not a smoky medieval foundry. Metal and Thermal Transduction through Genesis Cards, engineered sigils and human precision tooling perform most repetitive metal-forming operations. A few anvils, manual tools and controlled hearths can remain, especially for apprentices/junior smiths practicing labor-intensive tasks. No coal-burning furnace is the dominant technological engine; bright smokeless controlled thermal effects can produce suitable heat, while their exact microphysics remain inherited. Workshop walls can bear tools and display armor/weapons, but the **architecture** (frontage and depth, two-tier plaza, stone and constrained sigils) matters more than specific merchandise. Greenery/water is much **more restrained than in the large Accord Registry or inn**. Avoid excessive wood, giant fountains and digitally projected blue control panes from rejected images.

# Blacksmith Row and artisan economy
LOCKED definition: **Blacksmith Row is not an industrial forge district. It is a professional artisan plaza where highly competent smiths demonstrate their mastery of matter through Transduction.**

Three prestigious Rows in Yellow District, exact locations open. Smaller smithy clusters and individual shops in Red/Orange/Yellow; generally no noisy smithing in Green/Blue/Violet. Green, Blue, Violet and exceptional White professional ranks operate on Row. Professional ranks distinct from districts and energy basins. Thermal and Metal Manipulation Genesis Cards dominate; some hammering and manual finishing remains. Illustrative ~60–65% process by Transduction at lower Row skill, up to ~100% for elite Violet/White; not universal physical efficiency. No continuously active furnaces in Row shops. Clean open showrooms with armor/weapons, commissions and observable artisanship. Smiths as conductors of matter.

Architecture: long rectangular central public plaza, working example ~300 meters, final dimension open. On each side large deeper ground-floor smithy suites face plaza. Above each side smaller but spacious upper suites are RECESSED behind an elevated promenade with protective railings. NO OVERHANG; promenade lies within ground-floor footprint. Ground floor prime real estate. Working range 8–16 suites per side per tier = 32–64 per Row, not final. Occupancy highly competitive, often decades long; vacancies provoke bidding; municipality captures premium property value. Exact leasing/auction procedure open.

Six author-approved CIVIC artisan domains (not AERA operational domains):
1. Metalworking and armorsmithing.
2. Textiles, clothing and leatherwork: distinguish material production (bolts, fibers, prepared leather) from finished tailoring, garments, shoes, saddlery. Distributed citywide; upscale boutiques Green/Blue/Violet; no required textile quarter.
3. Architecture and construction: architects, runesmiths, builders, stonemasons, on-site rune installation; distributed, no architects row.
4. Alchemy and biological materials: independent material-science alchemists, apothecaries, specialist houses.
5. Genesis technology and specialized Transduction: Cardsmiths, industrial Genesis Card manufacturing, maege-glass and apparatus.
6. Household goods and decorative arts: furniture, ceramics, glass, books, paper and homewares.
