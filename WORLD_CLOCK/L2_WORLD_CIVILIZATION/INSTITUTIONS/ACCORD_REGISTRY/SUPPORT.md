# Support Specialist certification: battlefield control

## Core identity
The **Guardian controls and occupies the ELDRIS**; the **Support Specialist controls the battlefield**. Support is the expedition's combat force multiplier: protects allies, modifies terrain and engagement geometry, funnels opponents into advantageous positions/kill boxes, controls hostile movement, preserves friendly maneuver, intercepts ranged Transduction, and selectively assists with emergency stabilization or opportunistic attacks. An Architect, Augmenter, maegi, Summoner, or Cardsmith is a Transduction practitioner; the Registry's combat role is determined by function, not interface. Support may have offensive or limited healing skills without becoming the Offensive Specialist or helkir. Personal shielding is an expected bread-and-butter competence, not a mandated named Binding for every station.

## Examination technology and procedure
- Universal Crown Accord Registry chamber: circular, radius 100 ft, diameter 200 ft; entire floor an instructor-controlled sigil.
- Thirteen individually activated consecutive scenarios; each projects hostile ELDRIS and allied combatant manifolds. Simulations are interactive realizations of recursive topology and can cause real harm to an actual candidate; safety protocols are still open.
- Each engagement has an executable choreographed structure, with randomized events, timings, attacks, routes and tactical opportunities so that rote memorization does not suffice. Difficulty and outcomes should remain comparable across randomized variants; exact calibration open.
- The full hostile-vs-ally combat choreography must run to its conclusion; the projected allied combatants must defeat/neutralize the required ELDRIS. Candidate intervention must support the allied objective. A fast kill by the candidate is not the primary scoring metric; support interventions, including an opportunistic Genesis beam, are permitted.
- **Every protected allied manifold must survive. Any allied manifold death = station Failed (0), overriding any average.** If all survive and the required engagement is completed, station color is based on the **accumulated average group damage across all protected allied manifolds** (not the worst-injured ally alone). White=no group damage; Violet=negligible average group damage; Blue=minor; Green=moderate; Yellow=significant; Orange=severe; Red=critical average group damage with every ally surviving; Failed=any ally death or failure of the required engagement. These descriptive grades are a *working calibration*, not exact numeric injury thresholds. Whether to score peak trauma or end-state trauma is OPEN; do not silently decide. A support candidate may locally stabilize an ally but is not necessarily a full helkir.
- Thirteen station scores 0–7; all thirteen must be completed to receive any overall certification rating. Completed rating is floor(sum of thirteen scores / 13), below 1 = Failed, White requires all thirteen White. Genesis fatigue is cumulative.

## Category I — One allied combatant against one ELDRIS (tests 1–6)
1. **Red ELDRIS — Foundational Protection.** One allied manifold vs one Red hostile. Randomized attack sequence tests whether candidate can apply and maintain a personal shield while allowing ally to defeat the hostile. Alternative valid interventions permitted. Outcome: average group damage.
2. **Orange ELDRIS — Adaptive Shielding.** One ally vs Orange hostile. Changes of angle, timing and approach challenge shield placement/renewal and the ally's maneuverability. Outcome: average group damage.
3. **Yellow ELDRIS — Ranged Transduction Interception.** One ally vs Yellow hostile. Hostile Transduction pressure challenges barriers and shielding while preserving an allied attack route. Do not require a particular spell. Outcome: average group damage.
4. **Green ELDRIS — Terrain and Movement Advantage.** One ally vs Green hostile. Candidate may shape earthen features, icy movement constraints, short tactical corridors, barriers or other legitimate battlefield controls to create a finishing opportunity. Outcome: average group damage.
5. **Blue ELDRIS — Combined Protection and Positioning.** One ally vs Blue hostile. Multiple shifting attack vectors and counter-positioning require dynamic protection, safe approach and tactical intervention. Outcome: average group damage.
6. **Violet ELDRIS — Adaptive High-Complexity Support.** One ally vs Violet hostile. Variable threat pattern and pressure require sustained effective support through a complete engagement. Outcome: average group damage.

## Category II — Two allies: Guardian + Offensive Specialist (tests 7–10)
7. **Static Group I — Establish the Engagement.** Two allied manifolds (one Guardian, one Offensive Specialist) face an ELDRIS group. Candidate protects both and creates useful battlefield geometry without obstructing allies. Outcome: average group damage.
8. **Static Group II — Funnel and Kill Box.** Two allies vs more demanding static ELDRIS group. Candidate can create barriers/earthen berms and favorable approach lanes to help Guardian occupy threats and Offensive Specialist neutralize them. Methods unrestricted. Outcome: average group damage.
9. **Dynamic Group I — Moving Containment and Mobility.** Two allies vs moving hostile group. Candidate adapts shielding, terrain and/or localized spatial transitions to keep the team operational. Outcome: average group damage.
10. **Dynamic Group II — Simultaneous Combat Support.** Two allies vs complex moving hostile group; overlapping threat directions require prioritization, sustained protection and field modification. Outcome: average group damage.

## Category III — Full projected allied expedition party (tests 11–13)
11. **Full-Party Engagement — Distributed Shields.** Full allied party vs ELDRIS group; candidate coordinates protective coverage across multiple allies and supports the party's successful elimination of hostiles. Exact party size OPEN (seven is an illustrative expedition size, not a locked exam count). Outcome: average group damage.
12. **Multi-Directional Engagement — Battlefield Restructuring.** Full party vs moving hostile threats from different directions. Candidate may create walls, dynamic ice fields, screens, shield layers and tactical repositioning opportunities; aim to preserve ally function. Outcome: average group damage.
13. **Integrated Combat Support — Changing Battle.** Full party vs high-complexity, randomized multi-phase ELDRIS engagement. Candidate combines shielding, terrain control, selective assistance, possible emergency stabilization and/or opportunistic hostile elimination to help allies complete the full engagement. Outcome: average group damage.

## Important distinction: fixed objectives, flexible means
- A candidate is not forced to use earth, ice, barriers, Cards, summons, spatial corridors, or a Genesis beam in any particular test. These are illustrative possible solutions.
- The projected ally must be able to complete the combat objective, and support must not sabotage its movement or attacks.
- The candidate is graded by ally survival and injury, not by a checklist of techniques or personal damage output.
- Guardian tests score spatial ELDRIS containment; Offensive tests score elimination intervals; Helkir tests score biological coherence; Support tests score average accumulated allied-group trauma, with any allied death forcing station Failed (0).



## Open design decisions
- Exact trauma-to-color thresholds; precise damage normalization and aggregation across allies; whether accumulated damage measures peak injury, damage events, or end-state injury (do not silently choose); whether in-trial stabilization changes the recorded damage.
- Exact randomized choreography algorithms, comparable difficulty calibration, enemy group composition and full-party headcount.
- Explicit candidate injury and medical intervention rules for Support exams (do not automatically import all Guardian-specific medical rules).
- Whether every candidate must demonstrate a personal shield as an entrance requirement, or whether other equally effective protections qualify.
- Full classification of support-eligible MK157 Bindings and their canon status. Some MK157 catalogue entries are ALPHA candidates, not automatically locked.
