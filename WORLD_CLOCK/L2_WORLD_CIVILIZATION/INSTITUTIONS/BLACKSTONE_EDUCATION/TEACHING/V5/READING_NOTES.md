## T02 · topology brace ambiguity — explicit interpretation

The repeated conventional example prints X={a,b,c} and asks whether `{{∅,{a},X}}` is a topology. Taken literally, that outer singleton family is **not** a topology on X: it does not contain ∅ and X as open-set members. If the doubled braces are a formatting artifact and the intended family is `τ={∅,{a},X}`, that family **is** a topology: the three sets are nested, and arbitrary unions and finite intersections stay in the family. Both readings are documented; the package does not supply an author-approved correction selecting one. Do not teach the literal singleton as a valid topology. The basic set/subset illustration is available in Week 1; proving topology axioms needs their introduction, not an assumption that later lessons were already taught.

This finite-set check uses the usual definition, independently checked against [MIT topology notes, Definition 7.25](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-s097-applied-category-theory-january-iap-2019/03fd10e5957c6e5c879b5b46aebee333_18-s097iap19ch7.pdf). That is an external verification reference, not a selected in-world textbook.
