# OR-01 — Violet Dungeon staging settlement: geography, zoning and access

**Orange arc of Book Red · 2026-11-08.** ## Identity and geometry

The Violet Dungeon staging settlement is a permanent, materially and economically developed mini-city serving a Violet-stratum dungeon, distinct from the larger civilian city. Its author-selected **working footprint is a square of approximately 2 miles × 2 miles = 4 square miles**. It is not a temporary tent encampment. The related large city occupies an approximately **10 mile × 10 mile** square. Their **working geographic separation is approximately five miles**, not seven miles: the user explicitly corrected a seven-mile slip caused by confusion with the Blue Arc setting. The five-mile reference endpoints and precise actual walking route are not yet mapped; traveling from Kaira's apartment inside the enormous city adds non-negligible intra-city distance. Do not replace this with exact commute time, straight-line street geometry or a fabricated satellite map.

The dungeon entrance occupies the northern, slightly north-offset part of the settlement, not the center or a corner. The cave/sinkhole is naturally formed, later excavated and enlarged, and has winding/spiraling descent. Prior concept illustrations exaggerate the opening; no mile-wide hole, final entrance dimensions or depth count adopted. The settlement is built within an advanced stone/sigil civilization; entrances and public streets are constructed rather than muddy wilderness tracks.

## Housing and length of stay

Permanent residents, workshop owners, expert craftspeople, returning aithren, vaelum groups, and research parties can rent homes or apartments for months or years: **six months, one year, two years** are author illustrations, not fixed lease terms. Private residences may be very spacious, especially in the northeast premium area. Neither NW nor NE exact dimensions are locked: the user estimated around two-thirds of a mile square each while viewing the schematic, explicitly provisional and constrained by central dungeon land. Do not assign both corners exact mathematical area from that estimate.

Inns support short-term lodging: groups gather and provision for a few nights, spend a period descending into the dungeon (the existing repository illustrates roughly two weeks), return, stay a few nights for repairs and rest, and may dive again or depart. A small to medium inn may have **approximately 11 to the low 20s guest rooms**. Maximum local building height selected for the working settlement is **four stories total (ground + three upper stories)**. An inn's fourth floor could offer larger premium or prolonged-stay rooms; **this is a design option, not a compulsory floor-plan rule**. Private apartments and residence buildings can have different arrangements. Ru’ne-based elevator systems exist in user-supplied `Elevators.pdf`, but that PDF explicitly applies **GROUND CITIES ONLY**; do not automatically universalize or require every staging-inn installation without author confirmation of the settlement's inclusion in that category.

## Land use and community character

The central civic plaza can include maintained landscaping, parkland and social hangouts; the Registry itself is an institutional but socially active anchor. Southwest artisans and southeast shops make the settlement livable as a permanent city. Continuous recovery of Genesis Dust, Genesis crystals, vaen and naturally grown resources from dungeon expeditions supports skilled services, hospitality, commerce and research. The entire settlement is a working community: research groups, vaelum, independent aithren, local residents, restaurants, shopping, metalwork, lodging and regulated resources. Not a medieval camp; not exclusively a royal palace-scale monumental environment.

### Location premium and expedition economy

Proximity to the dungeon commands premium private housing and extended-stay rents for long-term delvers, wealthy visitors and resident artisans. Yellow/Green city-price comparisons are qualitative analogies, **not a rent table** or resident income rule. Inns and temporary/shared lodging coexist with expensive homes so ordinary aithren can stay; craftspeople may operate shops while living locally. Strong expedition demand raises commercial costs without establishing proportional worker wages.

Illustrative teams spend **two or three nights** assembling and provisioning, descend for **roughly two weeks**, then return to eat, sleep, repair, exchange information and resupply; a six-month private rental is another example, not every expedition's schedule. Storage rings/bracelets can help equipped groups, not every team; none is granted to Kaira before Red's ending. Hospitality and logistics are major economic activities. [The dungeon owner](../ENVIRONMENT/DUNGEONS.md) retains confinement/persistence and the working specific 17–23-mile depth, which is not route length or a travel-time formula.
