# Two aithren cultures: settled delvers and mobile storm chasers

**Project:** MK163  
**Status:** ESTABLISHED / INHERITED WITH AUTHOR AMENDMENTS  
**Canonical path:** `WORLD_CLOCK/L2_WORLD_CIVILIZATION/CULTURE/DUNGEON_DELVERS_AND_STORM_CHASERS.md`

Both cultures collect Genesis crystals, Genesis Dust from defeated eldris, and vaen. Their different environments—not moral worth or a compulsory occupational rank—produce different habits.

**Dungeon delvers:** Persistent geographic structures are mapped and known in general extent. The eldris cannot migrate between chromatic strata or escape the dungeon. Settlements at entrances therefore support comparatively protected staging, lodging, supplies, markets and long-term communities. The Crown benefits through trade, visitor traffic, taxes and managed resource access. These staging areas may become large. Relaxed life outside does not make the dungeon interior safe: inside, professional navigation, fatigue management and combat discipline matter.

Outside the dungeon the author imagines something like a festival/infield community and an early computer-engineering hobbyist culture: socializing, hacky-sack-like play, raeon, comparing Genesis builds and Shapings, discussion, experimentation, collaboration and friendship. Respect is earned through competence and contribution. ER Foam conversation circles are a source example of social/information-exchange surfaces.

**Storm chasers:** Teams travel into transient Genesis storms and domai in the Wilds. They face uncertain geography, dangerous energetic conditions, emergent eldris, no equivalent dungeon-stratum containment, harvest opportunities and extraction pressure. The author compares their preparation and execution with elite field operators and storm/tornado chasers. The analogy concerns discipline, not imported real-world ranks or equipment. They go in, do the work and get out.

The groups can clash culturally when they meet: one reads the other as careless, the other as rigid. Neither stereotype proves lower professional ability. They can coexist inside the same broad aithren identity or recognized organizational ecosystem without adding a second mandatory government hierarchy.

The King's Tournament intensifies the existing dungeon social environment. Competitors disappear into large dungeons for days, so the staging community becomes the spectator festival rather than a continuous view of underground racing.
