-------------------------------------------------------- 
CORE DEFINITION 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
Cartels are localized, homegrown power networks that operate OUTSIDE 
the formal sanctuary–crown–nobility power system. 
They are not sovereign powers, not rebels, and not conquerors. 
They are destabilization actors, market controllers, and leverage engines. 
Cartels manage power indirectly by exploiting dependency, vice, debt, 
blackmail, and asymmetric pressure rather than open force. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
POWER SCALE & ROLE 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Cartels are a NUISANCE-tier threat to the Crown (persistent, costly, hard to remove) 
- Cartels are a MAJOR threat to local noble houses 
- Cartels are NOT existential threats to sanctuaries or the Crown as a whole 
- Cartels cannot openly rival or overthrow the Crown or elevated sanctuaries 
A cartel may rival a noble house ONLY indirectly and ONLY at ground level, 
never through open military or political domination. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
RELATIONSHIP TO SANCTUARIES & THE CROWN 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Sanctuaries are elevated, fortified, and crown-controlled 
- Cartels do NOT operate inside official sanctuary authority structures 
- Cartels avoid direct confrontation with: 
  - Sanctuaries 
  - Crown military forces 
  - Open royal intervention 
Exposure is fatal to a cartel. 
Once clearly identified and provable, a cartel is vulnerable to eradication. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
CARTEL OPERATIONAL METHOD (FULCRUM DOCTRINE) 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
Cartels do not seize power. 
They destabilize load-bearing points within existing power structures. 
Primary method: FULCRUM DESTABILIZATION

Typical pattern: 
1. Identify a hidden dependency of a noble house or local power 
   - Debt 
   - Illicit income 
   - Trade reliance 
   - Political leverage 
   - Personal vice or secret 
2. Gain indirect control over that dependency 
3. Apply slow, deniable pressure 
4. Trigger internal collapse, disgrace, or default 
5. Collect compensation (land, rights, income, access) 
The noble house destroys itself. 
The cartel inherits value quietly. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
GEOGRAPHIC RULES 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Cartels are REGIONAL, not global 
- Cartels do not project power across the world 
- Cartels fight ONLY neighboring or adjacent rivals 
- Geography determines HOW a cartel operates, not whether it can exist 
Valid cartel environments include: 
- Inland regions 
- Coastal regions 
- Urban sanctuaries (externally) 
- Trade crossroads 
- Resource basins 
- Border zones 
A single region may host MANY overlapping cartels. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
STARTING ZONE CONFIGURATION (CANON INTENT) 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Main character’s starting region: 
  - 6 kingdoms (ground-level political units) 
  - ~11 active cartels operating concurrently 
- Cartels overlap in influence 
- No cartel has clean territorial ownership 
- Violence persists due to unresolved competition 
- The region is unstable by design 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
INTER-CARTEL DYNAMICS 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Cartels fight 1–3 neighboring cartels 
- Wars are localized, repetitive, and rarely decisive 
- Victory is denial, disruption, or leverage — not total control 
- Some regions are permanently contested

PROXY WARFARE DOCTRINE 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
Cartels prefer proxies and intermediaries to maintain deniability. 
Common proxies include: 
- Pirate lords 
- Smugglers 
- Criminal guild offshoots 
- Exiled nobles or heirs 
- Disgraced officials 
- Foreign agents 
All proxy alliances are TEMPORARY and TRANSACTIONAL. 
Proxies are disposable once objectives are achieved. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
DESTABILIZATION VECTORS 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
Cartels weaponize: 
- Drugs / addictive substances 
- Vice markets 
- Blackmail 
- Kidnapping 
- Financial entanglement 
- Information manipulation 
Example (canonical pattern): 
- Introduce illicit substance into sanctuary via proxy 
- Target high-leverage individual (e.g., prince) 
- Induce addiction, compromised judgment, or scandal 
- Force political missteps, disgrace, or leverage 
- Escalate to blackmail or abduction if needed 
The cartel never attacks the crown directly. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
VISIBILITY & ATTRIBUTION 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Cartels are widely suspected but rarely provable 
- Attribution exists without evidence 
- Outcomes are blamed on: 
  - Chance 
  - Incompetence 
  - Internal failure 
- Everyone “knows” it was a cartel 
- No one can prove which one 
Cartels are KNOWN SHADOWS, not hidden masters. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
PUBLIC PERCEPTION 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Common citizens know cartels exist 
- Names may be whispered, rarely confirmed

- Some cartels are feared more than others 
- Volatile, asymmetric cartels are treated as wild cards 
Fear is uneven and regional. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
CROWN RESPONSE DOCTRINE 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Crown tolerates cartels until intervention cost exceeds tolerance 
- Intervention is: 
  - Selective 
  - Symbolic 
  - Rarely decisive 
- Occasional eradication used as warning or stabilization 
- Total elimination is avoided due to: 
  - Economic shock 
  - Trade collapse 
  - Civil unrest 
  - Power vacuums 
This is risk management, not weakness. 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
WHAT CARTELS WANT (HARDLOCKED) 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Long-term income streams 
- Land access or rights 
- Resource control 
- Debt leverage 
- Trade monopolies 
Cartels do NOT want: 
- Crowns 
- Thrones 
- Public legitimacy 
- Open rule 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
NARRATIVE FUNCTION 
-------------------------------------------------------- 
- Persistent background instability 
- Source of deniable conflict 
- Political rot beneath visible order 
- Wild-card antagonists 
- Pressure mechanism for noble collapse 
- Environmental hazard rather than constant action threat
