# Manufacturing walkthrough, distinctions and examples

## A. Assisted Cardsmithing procedure
1. **Concept/design:** Decide the desired Card manifestation, relevant basin(s), scaffold, and resource requirements.
2. **Workbench setup:** Inspect a valid physical sigil composed of `ru’ne`; place blank maege-glass Card at the central violet-colored anchor; place appropriate `vaen`/Genesis crystals/dust at selected chromatic anchors.
3. **Activation:** The sigil establishes a constrained and sustained manufacturing field and exposes a coupled projected manifold for the Card.
4. **Geometric work:** Cardsmith manipulates the projected geometry with two engineered styluses, making, moving and connecting structures.
5. **Review:** Check that intended manifold connections are complete, coherent, admissible and appropriate for Card operation.
6. **Stabilization/identity resolution:** Instruct the manufacturing field to stabilize and resolve the designed topology and appropriate consumed resources into the Card's persistent identity.
7. **Deactivation:** Turn off the sigil; the projection disengages while the Card retains its new configuration.
8. **Testing:** The exact safe test protocol, failure modes and inspection method are OPEN, not a new author-locked mandatory stage in the universal six-step Shaping lifecycle.

This procedure describes **manufacturing**, not a replacement for AERA's six-stage Transduction lifecycle (`Conceive → Shape → Hold → Resolve → Manifest → Release`) during active Shaping.

## B. Direct native Cardsmithing procedure
1. Practitioner conceives the Card's required manifold and substrate coupling.
2. Practitioner directly accesses and constructs local Genesis-field topology without an externally prepared sigil.
3. Practitioner sustains and refines all geometric constraints personally.
4. Practitioner resolves/encodes the Card's persistent manifold using necessary effort/energy and any contextually required materials.
5. Practitioner disengages the manufacturing field, leaving the encoded Card.
Exact direct fabrication resource costs and physical mechanics remain OPEN.

## C. Why two routes coexist
- A good sigil provides a reliable generalized scaffold, lowers native control demands, and speeds repeatable manufacture.
- Excellent sigils and rare identity-bearing `vaen` can still produce advanced Cards.
- A native Architect can exceed a generic scaffold's design space, but must personally supply geometry, stability and precision.
- Direct construction can be slow, expensive and error prone; sigil-assisted craft is not low-status or intrinsically inferior.
- Example: a veteran sigil Cardsmith may produce a better standard Fireball Card than a novice native Architect; a master native practitioner may eventually produce an unusually customized fireball manifold unavailable from an ordinary generic sigil.
- The author floated an illustrative direct-card premium of ~2.3 times standard, not a universal economic constant.

## D. Distinguish three systems
- **Physical `ru’ne` and sigil:** durable inscribed geometric apparatus.
- **Projected coupled field/manifold:** active geometry maintained by the workbench during Card manufacture.
- **Card's encoded persistent topology:** the manufactured configuration retained after deactivation.
- **Native Architect `velis`:** temporary field topology directly formed by practitioner; not a synonym for a physical sigil.
