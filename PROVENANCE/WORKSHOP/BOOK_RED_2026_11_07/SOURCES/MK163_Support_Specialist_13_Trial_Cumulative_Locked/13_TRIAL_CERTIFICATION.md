# MK163 — Accord Registry Combat Support Specialist: 13-Trial Certification

**Status:** Author-approved examination architecture and combat role; trial-specific tactical pressures engineered as non-exclusive scenario examples. Exact trauma-to-color calibration and certain scenario details remain open. **This checkpoint is cumulative, not a verbatim transcript.**

## Core identity
The **Guardian controls and occupies the ELDRIS**; the **Support Specialist controls the battlefield**. Support is the expedition's combat force multiplier: protects allies, modifies terrain and engagement geometry, funnels opponents into advantageous positions/kill boxes, controls hostile movement, preserves friendly maneuver, intercepts ranged Transduction, and selectively assists with emergency stabilization or opportunistic attacks. An Architect, Augmenter, maegi, Summoner, or Cardsmith is a Transduction practitioner; the Registry's combat role is determined by function, not interface. Support may have offensive or limited healing skills without becoming the Offensive Specialist or helkir. Personal shielding is an expected bread-and-butter competence, not a mandated named Binding for every station.

## Examination technology and procedure
- Universal Crown Accord Registry chamber: circular, radius 100 ft, diameter 200 ft; entire floor an instructor-controlled sigil.
- Thirteen individually activated consecutive scenarios; each projects hostile ELDRIS and allied combatant manifolds. Simulations are interactive realizations of recursive topology and can cause real harm to an actual candidate; safety protocols are still open.
- Each engagement has an executable choreographed structure, with randomized events, timings, attacks, routes and tactical opportunities so that rote memorization does not suffice. Difficulty and outcomes should remain comparable across randomized variants; exact calibration open.
- The full hostile-vs-ally combat choreography must run to its conclusion; the projected allied combatants must defeat/neutralize the required ELDRIS. Candidate intervention must support the allied objective. A fast kill by the candidate is not the primary scoring metric; support interventions, including an opportunistic Genesis beam, are permitted.
- **Every protected allied manifold must survive. Any allied manifold death = station Failed (0).** If all survive and the required engagement is completed, station color is based on the **greatest trauma sustained by any protected allied manifold** (the worst-injured ally, not an average). White=none; Violet=negligible; Blue=minor; Green=moderate; Yellow=significant; Orange=severe; Red=critical/near-fatal but alive; Failed=death or failure of required engagement. These descriptive grades are a *working calibration*, not exact numeric injury thresholds. Whether to score peak trauma or end-state trauma is OPEN; do not silently decide. A support candidate may locally stabilize an ally but is not necessarily a full helkir.
- Thirteen station scores 0–7; all thirteen must be completed to receive any overall certification rating. Completed rating is floor(sum of thirteen scores / 13), below 1 = Failed, White requires all thirteen White. Genesis fatigue is cumulative.

## Category I — One allied combatant against one ELDRIS (tests 1–6)
1. **Red ELDRIS — Foundational Protection.** One allied manifold vs one Red hostile. Randomized attack sequence tests whether candidate can apply and maintain a personal shield while allowing ally to defeat the hostile. Alternative valid interventions permitted. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
2. **Orange ELDRIS — Adaptive Shielding.** One ally vs Orange hostile. Changes of angle, timing and approach challenge shield placement/renewal and the ally's maneuverability. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
3. **Yellow ELDRIS — Ranged Transduction Interception.** One ally vs Yellow hostile. Hostile Transduction pressure challenges barriers and shielding while preserving an allied attack route. Do not require a particular spell. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
4. **Green ELDRIS — Terrain and Movement Advantage.** One ally vs Green hostile. Candidate may shape earthen features, icy movement constraints, short tactical corridors, barriers or other legitimate battlefield controls to create a finishing opportunity. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
5. **Blue ELDRIS — Combined Protection and Positioning.** One ally vs Blue hostile. Multiple shifting attack vectors and counter-positioning require dynamic protection, safe approach and tactical intervention. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
6. **Violet ELDRIS — Adaptive High-Complexity Support.** One ally vs Violet hostile. Variable threat pattern and pressure require sustained effective support through a complete engagement. Outcome: worst ally trauma.

## Category II — Two allies: Guardian + Offensive Specialist (tests 7–10)
7. **Static Group I — Establish the Engagement.** Two allied manifolds (one Guardian, one Offensive Specialist) face an ELDRIS group. Candidate protects both and creates useful battlefield geometry without obstructing allies. Outcome: most severely injured ally.
8. **Static Group II — Funnel and Kill Box.** Two allies vs more demanding static ELDRIS group. Candidate can create barriers/earthen berms and favorable approach lanes to help Guardian occupy threats and Offensive Specialist neutralize them. Methods unrestricted. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
9. **Dynamic Group I — Moving Containment and Mobility.** Two allies vs moving hostile group. Candidate adapts shielding, terrain and/or localized spatial transitions to keep the team operational. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
10. **Dynamic Group II — Simultaneous Combat Support.** Two allies vs complex moving hostile group; overlapping threat directions require prioritization, sustained protection and field modification. Outcome: worst ally trauma.

## Category III — Full projected allied expedition party (tests 11–13)
11. **Full-Party Engagement — Distributed Shields.** Full allied party vs ELDRIS group; candidate coordinates protective coverage across multiple allies and supports the party's successful elimination of hostiles. Exact party size OPEN (seven is an illustrative expedition size, not a locked exam count). Outcome: worst ally trauma.
12. **Multi-Directional Engagement — Battlefield Restructuring.** Full party vs moving hostile threats from different directions. Candidate may create walls, dynamic ice fields, screens, shield layers and tactical repositioning opportunities; aim to preserve ally function. Outcome: worst ally trauma.
13. **Integrated Combat Support — Changing Battle.** Full party vs high-complexity, randomized multi-phase ELDRIS engagement. Candidate combines shielding, terrain control, selective assistance, possible emergency stabilization and/or opportunistic hostile elimination to help allies complete the full engagement. Outcome: worst ally trauma.

## Important distinction: fixed objectives, flexible means
- A candidate is not forced to use earth, ice, barriers, Cards, summons, spatial corridors, or a Genesis beam in any particular test. These are illustrative possible solutions.
- The projected ally must be able to complete the combat objective, and support must not sabotage its movement or attacks.
- The candidate is graded by ally survival and injury, not by a checklist of techniques or personal damage output.
- Guardian tests score spatial ELDRIS containment; Offensive tests score elimination intervals; Helkir tests score biological coherence; Support tests score allied trauma.
