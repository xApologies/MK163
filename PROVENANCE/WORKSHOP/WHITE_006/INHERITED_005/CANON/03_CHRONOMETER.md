# Maege glass chronometer — author-described design

**Protected material name:** `maege glass` (not ordinary “mage glass”). **Sigil primitive:** `ru’ne`; ER Foam is Einstein–Rosen Foam, a projected tactile phase-field, not a physical rubber button.

A clear, solid rectangular prism approximately **3 × 3 × 8 inches**. Its **five non-front faces** are covered with tiny, square, orthogonal `ru’ne` that form **one integrated complete sigil across the five faces**. The long **front face is clear, with no `ru’ne` or sigil work**. Numbers appear by projection **inside the prism**, just behind that clear front surface, not as an external hologram, electronic display or projecting outward. Only elapsed numerical fields appear; no decorative UI or conventional digital-clock panel. Values may show days/hours/minutes/seconds as needed; a day is 31 hours.

On top, two extremely thin **projected ER Foam tactile fields** sit flush on the glass, not raised physical buttons. Inactive they appear dull opaque white and have a firm-rubber-like slight give. **Start/Stop** pad turns green while running, returns opaque white when stopped. **Reset** pad glows red while the display is active; pressing clears and deactivates display, and it returns to opaque white. Three separate chronometers provide redundancy. No invented mathematical mechanism for ER Foam.

The user accepted a **provisional visual** after several unsuccessful renders, but its limitations must not override these construction rules. The device is fantasy sigil technology, not Earth electronics.
