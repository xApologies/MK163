# Vaen merchant inspection, inventory, and basin bypass

## Merchant arrangement
A vaen shop has two broad inventory categories: (1) **single-basin vaen**, Red through Violet, whose known interaction is only one basin; and (2) **hybrid vaen**, likewise shelved under their **primary** Red–Violet basin, with additional **secondary** interactions. A hybrid with primary Green and secondary Red/Orange is shelved under **Hybrid → Green**. This does not mean secondary connections are absent.

A typical merchant may have an approximately 12-foot counter, glass showcase portions and a central checkout area with an embedded **Genesis-field inspection sigil**. A customer places one vaen specimen on that counter sigil; the merchant activates it; a coupled projection reveals its existing Genesis-field topology and primary/secondary interactions. This is a non-destructive inspection, not automatic interpretation, basin optimization or proof of later Card compatibility. Customer expertise matters. The 12-foot size is illustrative, not a universal code.

## Proposed author-selected mechanism
The **single-basin vaen** provides the topology/density reference for the basin being bypassed; the **hybrid vaen** couples that reference to the remaining active basins. Within the prepared Card manifold, energy density from the retained active basin connections is concentrated and structurally stabilized to emulate the bypassed basin's contribution (**local pseudo-basin density**). It does **not** secretly activate, draw from, or occupy the bypassed personal basin. The vaen are anchors, not unlimited fuel; energy draw, fatigue, throughput, integrity, and admissibility remain constraints. This is in-world engineering canon selection; exact equations and quantitative conversion thresholds are not yet fixed.

The optimized Card sets are disjoint: Fireball {Red,Blue}, Shield {Orange,Yellow,Green}; their intersection is empty and Violet is unoccupied. They can be active together subject to operator bandwidth, fatigue and control; the bypassed Orange and Blue references are *not* additional occupied basins. Fewer occupied basins do not automatically mean less total energy consumption.

Do not turn these provisional catalogue baseline assignments into independently derived topology or assume all Shapings admit identical compression. The six-step AERA Transduction lifecycle remains Conceive → Shape → Hold → Resolve → Manifest → Release. A Shaping is an operation; an operational Genesis Card is one interface.
