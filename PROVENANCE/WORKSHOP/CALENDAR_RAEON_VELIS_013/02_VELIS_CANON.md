# Velis — user-confirmed canonical definition

**Protected spelling:** velis (V-E-L-I-S); plural usage as appropriate. Do not transcribe as vellis or substitute another term.

A velis is a **naturally forming, locally stabilized configuration of the Genesis Field that functions as an anchor**. Velis occur without a practitioner deliberately constructing them. They are naturally occurring structural patterns, comparable in function and discoverability to natural sigils.

Historically, practitioners studied natural velis, their topology, and their stable relationships. This observation supplied the foundation for deliberately engineered ru’ne and sigil geometries. Natural velis are **not** written symbols or necessarily visible glyphs; they are Genesis Field structures. The underlying Genesis geometry precedes the human-engineered representations.

An Architect may construct an **artificial analogue or engineered local anchor**; distinguish this from a naturally occurring velis. If prior AERA documentation calls practitioner-created geometry a velis, preserve the source and explicitly distinguish natural origin from artificial realization in MK163; do not rewrite the upstream AERA repository without separate authorization. Do not claim all natural velis are identical or that all sigils are literal copies of a single velis.

Update the MK163 World Clock definitions, the first research expedition (Days 920–923 and 927–930), and any active indexes that currently treat natural velis as an unresolved equivalence. Preserve the research program's focus on natural ru’ne/sigil/anchor structures.
